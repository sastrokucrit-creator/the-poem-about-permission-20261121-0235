## #1 — "Scissors" (sans) — 928,645 — 279 chars
> I asked my mother for the scissors.
> She held them out, then kept them,
> turning the handles to my palm,
> blades shut, aimed at her own wrist.
>
> Take it, she said.
>
> For years I thought the gift was the scissors.
> It was her hand, staying open
> long enough for mine to close.

## #2 — "The Weight" (skypiiee) — 924,078 — 225 chars
> Can I hold her? I asked,
> and you laid her in my arms
> like a bird asleep.
>
> She weighed almost nothing.
> She weighed everything.
>
> When I gave her back,
> my arms stayed curved,
> still holding the shape
> of being trusted.

## #3 — "The Rinse" (kiencx) — 924,078 — 268 chars
> May I wash your hair?
> Just rinse it, she says.
> The soap I can do.
>
> She lathers it herself,
> elbows trembling,
> then leans back over the sink
> and lets her neck go:
>
> the whole weight of her head
> in my hands,
> the way she held mine
> when I was too new

## What wins (shared pattern)
- All three are ONE small, concrete, domestic human moment: a title + a single scene.
- All three open with an ASK ("I asked my mother…", "Can I hold her?", "May I wash your hair?").
- All three are about a VULNERABLE trust: sharp scissors, a newborn, an old mother's neck.
- All three land a TURN at the end that redefines what was given: the gift was the open
  hand, not the scissors; the arms keep the shape of being trusted; the care reverses
  across a lifetime (she held my head when I was too new to be asked).
- Exact physical detail carries the feeling: handles turned to the palm, blades shut,
  arms curved, elbows trembling, a neck going slack. No abstract words.
- None of them says the words "permission" or "agency" — they SHOW it.
- Plain voice, no rhyme, short lines, 225–279 chars (all near the 280 cap).

## My concept — "The Yes" (original, not a copy)
- Opposite side of the same coin: the GRANTER, not the asker. All three leaders are
  told from the asker's side; mine is told from the one who says yes and watches the
  yes leave.
- The turn: the word "yes" becomes a thing that walks away in her shoes — permission,
  once given, is no longer yours; it becomes the other person's steps.
- No object handed over (no scissors, no baby, no hair) — the gift is a WORD, and the
  image is sound (listening) rather than touch. Different sense, different side.
- No line, image or structure copied from any of the three: no open hand, no curved
  arms, no weight, no reversal across a lifetime. The "smaller and smaller yes" is my
  own image.
- Rejected draft: "The Key" (a mother pressing a warm key into a palm; "The house was
  never the gift. The gift was the click.") — REJECTED because its closing turn is a
  structural copy of #1's "For years I thought the gift was the scissors. It was her
  hand…". The Yes avoids that shape entirely.
